# 2026-09-27: real-time 1080p60 HEVC transcode, cards and host together

Host: Ryzen 7 5800X, kernel 7.2.6-1-cachyos. Cards: card 0 (3120A,
0000:2f:00.0) and card 1 (0000:24:00.0), each with Intel-Phi-AVX512's
`phi-vpu-worker` resident (card 1 had 647 to 673 MiB available beside it,
card 0 about 3.6 GiB). Software on every device: FFmpeg n9.0.2 and x265
4.2, unmodified; C build on the cards, SIMD build on the host.

## Result

`phifmpeg transcode bbb60-300s-closed.mkv out.mkv` (defaults: latency
150 s, 2 s segments, x265 ultrafast, CRF 28). Input: Big Buck Bunny 1080p60
(see the encode baseline record for its source), 300 s from 60 s in,
re-encoded on the host with closed GOPs every 120 frames: 18,000 frames,
150 segments.

| device | slots | segments | frames | share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| card 0 | 5 x (x265 pools=28, 560 MiB) | 24 | 2,880 | 16.0% |
| card 1 | 1 small (pools=14, 420 MiB) | 5 | 600 | 3.3% |
| host | 2 x (x265 pools=8) | 121 | 14,520 | 80.7% |

**0 deadline misses, 0 host backups, every card attempt used.** The last
segment was done 379.9 s after the video began arriving (300 s of video
plus the tail inside the 150 s budget). Card speed estimates at the end:
1.80 fps per slot on card 0, 1.63 on card 1's small slot, so card 0 as a
whole sustained about 9 fps.

## Verified

- The output decodes with no error and has 18,000 frames.
- PSNR against the source, every frame (`psnr` filter): card-encoded
  frames 39.69 dB mean, host-encoded 39.79 dB (different content).
- The same segments encoded by card and host (host re-encoded three of the
  card's segments with the same settings):

  | segment | card bytes | card PSNR | host bytes | host PSNR |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 0 | 158,658 | 40.295 | 158,972 | 40.357 |
  | 1 | 91,952 | 42.705 | 92,184 | 42.719 |
  | 2 | 110,293 | 42.090 | 109,881 | 42.080 |

  Parity: within 0.06 dB and 0.4 percent. The small differences are
  expected: x265's integer primitives are exact in C and SIMD alike, but
  its rate control uses floating point (x87 on the card, SSE on the host).

## How it got there (each run a full 300 s or 60 s transcode)

| run | change | cards' share | wasted card attempts | misses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (60 s) | one `phi run` per card slot, latency 60 | 0% | 8 of 8 | 0 |
| 2 (60 s) | latency 120 | 3.3% | 4 of 5 | 0 |
| 3 (60 s) | card runner, latency 90 | 26.7% (inflated by the tail) | 0 | 0 |
| 4 (300 s) | 6 lean slots, minimum-bound learning | 5.3% | 8 of 16 | 0 |
| 5 (300 s) | 5 slots, latency 150, softened learning | 11.3% | 12 of 30 | 0 |
| 6 (300 s) | idle slots only, estimates x1.2, card 1 small slot | **19.3%** | **0 of 29** | 0 |

Findings along the way, each measured:

1. **The stack serves one control session per card at a time.** Three
   concurrent 5 s `phi run`s take 15.1 s. (An earlier test that seemed to
   show concurrency was wrong: its background runs were in subshells, so
   `wait` never waited for them.) With a `phi run` held open per encoder,
   every `put`, `get` and kill waited: one `put` of 0.6 MB took 159 s.
   Fix: `phifmpeg-card`, a runner started once per card that works from a
   job directory, with the host making only short visits.
2. **Host load does not slow the cards.** Four card encoders with the host
   saturated took 53.7 to 58.4 s, against 52.9 to 58.4 s with it idle.
3. **Card slots are bound by memory, not compute.** One 1080p ultrafast
   encoder: 640 MB with 4 decode threads and x265's defaults; 513 MB with
   2 decode threads, `pools=28`, `frame-threads=2`, equally fast (37.1 s
   per 120 frames); 414 MB with `pools=14` (52.9 s).
4. **Card 0's ceiling in C is about 9.3 fps**: 5 lean slots 9.34 fps, 4
   standard slots 8.22 fps. Pinning each slot to its own 11 cores made it
   worse (8.40): the kernel knows the core topology (core k = CPUs
   1+4k..4+4k) and balances better than a static split. x265's `pmode`
   and `pme` do not help at ultrafast (40.9 s, 40.5 s, both 53.8 s).
5. **Queueing behind busy card slots causes a cascade.** Card encode times
   for equal-length segments spread from 57 to 85 s; a queued segment
   inherits the spread twice, runs late, its host backup wins, the card
   copy is cancelled after a full encode, and the next one starts late.
   Placing segments only on idle slots loses nothing, because a new
   segment arrives every 2 s.

## What the cards cannot do yet

At 1080p60 the cards' share is bounded by their C speed: about 9 fps from
card 0 and about 2 from card 1's single small slot. The levers left:

- **Card 1's memory.** With the AVX-512 worker's 2.7 GB freed, card 1
  would hold five slots like card 0 (another ~9 fps, about 15 percent).
  Not done: the worker belongs to another project and stays unless the
  owner says otherwise.
- **The vector unit.** On one host thread x265's SSE2 code is 3.0x its C
  code, SSE4 4.8x, AVX2 6.5x; the cards run C.

Open anomaly: once, a `phi run` of `chmod` returned exit status 101 with no
output; it did not reproduce by hand. Idempotent control commands are
retried.
